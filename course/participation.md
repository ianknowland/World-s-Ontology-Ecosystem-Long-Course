# Asynchronous Participation

Participation is a graded form of written intellectual engagement. It is not based on attendance or on the number of comments posted.

Students complete **nine required participation artifacts**:

- four peer reviews of classmates’ project pull requests, one for each of Projects 1–4; and
- five structured reflections on lecture debates, one for each recorded lecture set.

Together, these activities constitute the 30% Asynchronous Participation category in the syllabus.

## Required Pull-Request Reviews

For each of Projects 1–4, submit at least one substantive review of another student’s project pull request.

A substantive review must:

1. identify at least one substantive strength;
2. identify at least one meaningful modeling, conceptual, logical, methodological, or presentation question or concern;
3. explain the reasoning behind the concern; and
4. recommend a concrete improvement when a change is warranted.

A brief approval-only comment, generic praise, or a comment that supplies no reasoning does not satisfy the requirement.

See [`peer-review.md`](peer-review.md).

## Five Debate Reflections

Across the five recorded lecture sets, participants engage in several debates about ontology, modeling practice, artificial intelligence, and the future of the field.

For each lecture set, submit one structured reflection in the corresponding GitHub Discussion. Each reflection must:

1. identify the issue under dispute;
2. reconstruct the competing positions charitably;
3. identify an important assumption, distinction, or point of disagreement;
4. defend your own reasoned assessment; and
5. explain what evidence, argument, or clarification could change your assessment.

The goal is not to summarize everything said in the recording. Focus on one genuine point of disagreement and show that you understand why the disagreement matters.

## Participation Quality

Strong participation is charitable, specific, textually or technically grounded, and useful to another person. Students may disagree sharply with an argument or model provided they explain the basis for the disagreement.
