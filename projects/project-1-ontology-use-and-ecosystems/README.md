# Project 1 — Ontology Use and Ecosystems

**Due:** Week 3  
**Submission:** Pull request  
**Purpose:** Decide whether ontology engineering is actually appropriate for a problem and situate the problem within the contemporary ontology ecosystem.

## Task

Choose a real or realistic interoperability, data-integration, information-management, scientific, operational, or knowledge-representation problem. Do not begin by assuming that an ontology is the correct solution.

Write an analysis that answers the following.

### 1. Problem

Describe the problem in concrete terms. Identify the people, organizations, systems, or communities affected by it.

### 2. Why ontology might help

Explain what an ontology could contribute. Distinguish ontology-specific benefits from benefits that could be obtained by a database schema, controlled vocabulary, taxonomy, data dictionary, API contract, or ordinary software engineering.

### 3. Why ontology might **not** help

Identify at least two reasons ontology engineering could be unnecessary, disproportionate, or the wrong tool.

### 4. Scope and competency questions

State a provisional scope and write **5–8 competency questions**. Each question should be something a successful representation ought to help answer.

### 5. Ecosystem scan

Identify at least **three** relevant ontology, vocabulary, standards, foundry, or governance efforts. For each, explain:

- what it covers;
- why it might be relevant;
- whether you would reuse, align, extend, or avoid it; and
- what additional investigation would be required before adopting it.

### 6. Recommendation

Conclude with one of the following:

- **PROCEED** — ontology engineering appears justified;
- **PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS** — ontology may help, but only under stated constraints; or
- **DO NOT PROCEED** — another representation or engineering approach is more appropriate.

Defend the recommendation.

## Evaluation

Strong work:

- distinguishes an actual problem from a vague desire to “use ontology”;
- treats ontology as one engineering option rather than a default answer;
- writes useful competency questions;
- shows awareness of existing ecosystems and governance;
- makes explicit assumptions and tradeoffs; and
- reaches a defensible recommendation.
