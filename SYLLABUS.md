# PHI 605 SEM — The World’s Ontology Ecosystem

**Instructors:** John Beverley & Barry Smith  
**Format:** Fully asynchronous and remote

## Course Description

The World’s Ontology Ecosystem is a semester-long introduction to the foundations, methods, applications, and future of applied ontology. The course begins with ontology’s philosophical origins and the institutional landscape of contemporary ontology engineering, then moves through professional tradecraft and careful modeling practice, the relationship between ontology and artificial intelligence, ontology-enabled AI workflows, and competing visions for the field’s future.

The course treats ontology engineering as a practical discipline. Students examine major ontology ecosystems and foundry efforts, learn a repeatable process for deciding when ontology is useful and for modeling difficult domains, evaluate claims about AI, use LLMs as controlled support tools, and investigate semantic pipelines that connect language models with ontology artifacts.

## Assessment

- **30% — Asynchronous Participation:** required reviews of classmates’ pull requests plus five structured reflections on Smith–Beverley debates.
- **30% — Projects 1–4:** pull-request projects.
- **40% — Project 5:** capstone synthesis.

## Required Participation

- Submit at least one substantive review of another student’s pull request for each of Projects 1–4.
- Submit five structured debate reflections, one for each recorded lecture set, using GitHub Discussions.

## Projects

1. **Ontology Use and Ecosystems** — evaluate whether ontology engineering is appropriate for a problem and situate it within the broader ecosystem.
2. **Careful Modeling** — apply ontology tradecraft, design patterns, definitions, OWL, and reasoning to difficult cases.
3. **AI and Ontology** — evaluate and repair an AI-generated ontology artifact.
4. **Semantic Pipelines and Validation** — construct and evaluate an ontology-grounded AI workflow using explicit validation criteria.
5. **Capstone Synthesis** — integrate the major themes of the course in a substantial final project.
